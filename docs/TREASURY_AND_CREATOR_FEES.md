# Treasury epochs and creator-fee economics

**Status:** treasury state and epoch-disbursement instructions are implemented in the local Anchor crate on the current PR branch; the fee router is currently a tested math primitive only. No launch swap currently calls it, no SOL has been collected, no treasury has been deployed, and no distribution has occurred.

This document defines the exact interpretation used by the first implementation slice and separates it from the existing fixed WELP fee policy.

## 1. Three different fee concepts

Do not combine these into one number or charge them twice.

### 1.1 Existing fixed WELP platform fee policy

The existing policy in [FEES_AND_TREASURY.md](./FEES_AND_TREASURY.md) remains separate:

| Supported WELP operation | Fixed fee | Developer/operator | WELP treasury |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Supported trade | $0.10 USDC | $0.06 | $0.04 |
| Supported transfer | $0.10 USDC | $0.06 | $0.04 |
| Launch creation | $0.50 USDC | $0.30 | $0.20 |

That fee policy is not currently collected on-chain. It must not silently be applied on top of another fee for the same operation without explicit user disclosure and an agreed policy revision.

### 1.2 Creator-configured trading fee

Each launch can choose a creator fee from **0 to 300 basis points**:

- 0 bps = 0.00%
- 100 bps = 1.00%
- 300 bps = 3.00% (maximum)

The configured percentage is applied to eligible swap volume that passes through a WELP-controlled swap instruction. This fee is not a fee on every ordinary SPL token transfer on Solana. WELP cannot intercept arbitrary transfers that do not invoke WELP or a token extension hook.

### 1.3 Treasury share of the creator fee

The wording "0.5% of the fee" is implemented literally:

1. Calculate the creator fee from the eligible swap volume.
2. Route **0.5% of that creator-fee amount** to the WELP Treasury PDA.
3. Route the remainder of that creator-fee amount to creator rewards.

It is **not** 0.5% of gross swap volume. It is also not an additional 0.5 percentage-point fee on the user.

Example, for a 1.00 SOL eligible WELP-routed swap and a creator fee of 3.00%:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Eligible swap volume | 1.000000000 SOL |
| Creator fee (3.00%) | 0.030000000 SOL |
| Treasury share (0.5% of creator fee) | 0.000150000 SOL |
| Creator reward (99.5% of creator fee) | 0.029850000 SOL |

At a 0% creator fee, neither a creator reward nor a treasury share is generated by this route.

The exact on-chain path must calculate the gross fee and transfers in lamports with checked integer arithmetic. The TreasuryAccount stores a 0–9,999 remainder so fractions smaller than one lamport are carried across collections. This makes the accumulated treasury share equal to floor(0.5% of cumulative processed creator fees), rather than losing each tiny transaction's fractional share separately.

**Implementation status:** the Rust \`calculate_creator_fee_route\` helper and its tests are present. The buy/sell handler that validates a real swap, performs both SOL transfers atomically, increments the treasury counters and emits a receipt is not implemented yet. Therefore no fee routing is live.

## 2. Treasury PDA

The new local crate is \`programs/launchpad\`. Its \`TreasuryAccount\` is one deterministic PDA derived from \`["treasury-v1"]\` under the final launchpad program ID.

The account tracks:

- hardcoded developer wallet;
- cumulative treasury fees collected;
- cumulative creator-fee amount processed;
- cumulative developer disbursements;
- permanently retained reserve in lamports;
- last successful distribution timestamp;
- carried 0.5%-share remainder;
- PDA bump.

The developer address is compiled as:

\`\`\`text
ETuGxX6Znxr9q59THJtcYDJKt6RvCfN7wp79tRnbG1xG
\`\`\`

It is a public receiving address only. It is not a signing credential.

The implementation uses two PDAs:

- `TreasuryAccount` is the program-owned state PDA derived from `["treasury-v1"]`.
- `TreasuryVault` is a separate zero-data, System Program-owned PDA derived from `["treasury-vault-v1"]`. The state stores its address and bump. The launchpad signs the System Program transfer with those seeds during a matured disbursal.

The disbursement calculation preserves the vault's rent-exempt minimum and all previously locked reserve before considering available fee funds. The state PDA's rent is independent and is never counted as treasury revenue.

## 3. Fourteen-day epoch and 60/40 release

On treasury initialization, \`last_distribution_timestamp\` is set from \`Clock::get()?.unix_timestamp\`.

A successful distribution requires:

\[
now - last\_distribution\_timestamp \ge 1,209,600 \text{ seconds}
\]

This is a minimum fourteen-day lock. Exactly fourteen days is sufficient; later is also sufficient. If the clock value is earlier than the stored timestamp, the instruction fails closed.

### 3.1 How the 40% remains locked across repeated epochs

A literal split of the entire current vault balance at every epoch would eventually withdraw previously retained reserve. That would not be "permanently locked".

The implementation therefore keeps \`locked_reserve_lamports\` as a protected balance:

1. Compute current treasury lamports.
2. Subtract rent-exempt minimum.
3. Subtract all previously locked reserve.
4. Treat only the resulting **new, undistributed fees** as eligible for this epoch.
5. Transfer 60% of those eligible funds to the hardcoded developer wallet.
6. Add the remaining 40% to \`locked_reserve_lamports\`.
7. Set \`last_distribution_timestamp\` to current Solana clock time.

The next epoch cannot include earlier locked reserve in the distributable balance. There is no instruction in this crate to withdraw the reserve.

### 3.2 Permanent-lock caveat

No code should describe the retained reserve as permanently inaccessible while the program remains upgradeable by a privileged upgrade authority. Production release must either make the program immutable by revoking upgrade authority after independent review, or clearly disclose the exact multisig governance that can authorize upgrades. A future upgrade could otherwise change program behavior.

### 3.3 Distribution status

The following instructions are implemented in the local crate:

- \`initialize_treasury\`: permissionlessly creates the one Treasury PDA and initializes the developer address and epoch timestamp.
- \`disburse_treasury_funds\`: permissionless matured-epoch harvest, with an immutable destination constraint and a protected 40% reserve.

The treasury program ID in this branch is a sample local ID. It has no deployment keypair associated with it and must not be deployed. No user funds must be sent to a derived address from this build.

## 4. Raydium CPMM creator-fee compatibility

Raydium CPMM distinguishes its ordinary trade fee from its optional per-pool creator fee. The creator-fee percentage itself is read from \`AmmConfig.creator_fee_rate\`; it is shared by pools using that configuration. Pools can enable/disable it and choose which token side is charged, but cannot independently choose an arbitrary 0–3% rate when all pools use the same fee tier. Raydium's creator-fee share is a separate percentage of accrued creator fees, applied when the creator fee is collected. The current Raydium docs state that share is routed to Raydium's protocol-fee bucket, not automatically to the WELP Treasury PDA. See the [Raydium CPMM fee reference](https://github.com/raydium-io/raydium-docs-v1/blob/main/products/cpmm/fees.mdx).

Consequences for WELP:

- A WELP-only launch curve can enforce its configured creator fee in its own buy/sell instruction once implemented.
- Post-graduation swaps routed directly through Raydium CPMM do not execute WELP's buy/sell code.
- Raydium's current creator-fee rate is shared at the AMM configuration level; it is not an unrestricted per-pool 0–3% slider.
- Raydium's built-in creator-fee share does not deposit into WELP's Treasury PDA.
- An adapter must be designed and tested to collect Raydium creator-fee proceeds to a launch-controlled destination and atomically split the WELP treasury share from creator rewards. The exact pool initialization and creator-fee collection instruction versions, program ID, accounts, creator authority, side selection and rate configuration must be pinned before implementation.

Until that adapter is reviewed and tested, the UI must describe the 0–3% rate as a **planned WELP-routed creator fee**, not a universal post-graduation trading tax. Do not enable a token's fee slider in a launch mode where the selected route cannot enforce it.

Solana Token-2022 Transfer Hook can execute custom logic on token transfers, but that is a different token standard and requires an explicit hook program, extra-account-meta handling and AMM/wallet compatibility testing. It must not be switched on casually as a workaround. Reference: https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/transfer-hook

## 5. Other launch-economics constraints still unresolved

The current launch specification also proposes:

- maximum supply from 0 to 1,000,000,000 whole tokens (positive supply required to create a tradable launch);
- any decimal precision from 0 through 9;
- 80% of supply for the public sale and 20% reserved for liquidity;
- a fixed 85 SOL graduation milestone;
- linear pricing from 25 lamports per whole token to 187.5 lamports per whole token;
- a 0.02% creator initial purchase bundled with launch initialization.

The price endpoints and fixed 85 SOL cap are not consistent with every custom supply.

If the endpoints are interpreted as lamports per whole token, the linear curve's average price is:

\[
(25 + 187.5)/2 = 106.25 \text{ lamports per whole token}
\]

With an 80% public sale allocation, the maximum curve proceeds are approximately:

| Whole-token supply | Public sale allocation | Proceeds at the stated average price |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000,000,000 | 800,000,000 | 85 SOL |
| 200,000,000 | 160,000,000 | 17 SOL |
| 100,000,000 | 80,000,000 | 8.5 SOL |

Therefore, with these price endpoints, only a 1-billion-token launch can reach the 85 SOL milestone by selling the entire 80% public allocation. A creator choosing 200 million tokens would only raise approximately 17 SOL at those prices and could not graduate at a fixed 85 SOL cap.

Before implementing buy/graduation logic, one of these must be selected explicitly:
1. scale initial and terminal prices inversely with chosen supply to preserve the 85 SOL milestone;
2. scale the funding target proportionally with supply and keep the stated price endpoints;
3. keep the 85 SOL cap and accept that many smaller-supply launches cannot graduate.

The current UI and treasury crate do not choose among these alternatives. No launch will be enabled on an invented assumption.

The 0.02% initial buy must be an explicit, disclosed, creator-signed purchase instruction composed atomically after launch creation. It must count against the creator's own early-wallet limit and be charged the same fee as any other eligible purchase. It must not be a hidden privileged token allocation.

## Current implementation boundary

The local Anchor crate implements the treasury state account, initialization instruction, two-week disbursement instruction and tested fee/split math. It does **not** implement the buy/sell instruction, SOL fee transfers, launch minting, bonding curve, refunds, creator-fee CPI adapter, Raydium CPMM pool initialization, LP burn or a live mainnet service. This boundary is deliberate and must remain visible in the UI and release documentation.
